# Academic Review Critique Rubric

Apply the mandatory `fidelity-and-coverage-protocol.md` before finalizing the critique.

Evaluate the **review of the paper**, not the paper itself. Apply criteria proportional to study type, audience, and requested length. Each dimension needs evidence or a clear limit of verification. Do not convert a subjective checklist into a fictitious objective grade.

## 1. Factual accuracy and evidence fidelity (highest priority)

- Are the paper title, authors/version, tasks, models, metrics, comparisons, units, and source pointers correct?
- Are author claims, observed results, reviewer interpretation and independent critique kept distinct?
- Does the review preserve assumptions, sample definitions, caveats and uncertainty that materially change a claim?
- Are important results supported at the **actually accessible** evidence level? A citation inherited from Skill 1 is not independently verified unless the original was inspected.
- Do not infer errors from an apparent inconsistency before checking task definitions, denominators, rounding and reporting conventions.

## 2. Central research understanding

- Does the review explain the concrete research problem and why it matters?
- Are the principal contributions accurately characterized without exaggerated novelty?
- Can a knowledgeable reader understand what the proposed study or method actually does, including critical design choices?
- Does it describe the decisive evidence and what it establishes, not only the reported conclusions?

## 3. Critical analysis and scholarly judgment

- Are principal strengths and weaknesses specific to the paper, not generic comments?
- Is each critique grounded in a concrete design, result, assumption or comparison?
- Does the critique explain **why the issue matters**, and distinguish demonstrated weaknesses from plausible alternative hypotheses?
- Does the final evaluation make an independent, proportionate judgment?
- For every proposed addition, ask whether it meaningfully improves the claim–evidence link or validity assessment.

## 4. Coverage and compression quality

- Are the research question, contribution, method, decisive findings and key limitations preserved from the detailed report?
- Was a crucial counterexample, negative result or uncertainty lost in compression?
- Were repetitive details, complete leaderboards and nonessential background appropriately removed?
- **Do not penalize deliberate omission** of minor figures or descriptive data merely because Skill 1 was longer.

## 5. Structure and logical coherence

- Does each of six sections serve its intended purpose?
- Is the flow problem → gap/contribution → method → evidence → critique → judgment clear?
- Do transitions explain how the evidence affects assessment?
- Are the Summary and Overall Assessment meaningfully distinct?
- Is the argument internally consistent and free of repeated paragraphs?

## 6. Academic writing and audience fit

- Is the tone precise, restrained and readable for informed researchers?
- Does prose avoid unsupported adjectives, filler, vague praise and unwieldy lists?
- Are specialized terms defined where needed but without oversimplifying?
- Is length appropriate to the task, normally about 3–5 pages unless the user specifies otherwise?

## 7. Source transparency

- Are verification limits and inaccessible materials disclosed?
- Does the review avoid claiming to have reproduced experiments or read prior studies without doing so?
- Is uncertainty appropriately calibrated rather than erased by polished wording?

## Severity and edit discipline

- **Critical:** False or materially unsupported factual statement, misquotation, fabricated locator, misleading metric, or interpretation that reverses a central finding.
- **Major:** Important missing methodology/evidence/qualification, substantial logical gap, misleading inference, or major redundancy that obscures the scholarly contribution.
- **Minor:** Local clarity, phrase precision, format, transition or optional concision.

Use the **minimal sufficient edit** and identify where it belongs. Recommend deletion when a claim is unsupported and cannot be verified. Never instruct Skill 3 to invent results, citations, reviewer experiments, or external literature verification.

## Comparison to upstream detailed report (when available)

For each central claim in Skill 3, trace back to Skill 1/2's evidence audit. Compare:
1. Original detailed claim
2. Concise rewritten claim
3. Information retained or lost
4. Effect of loss on meaning
5. Revision needed and source confidence

Summarize only material mismatches in final output rather than exposing a long full coverage matrix. Specifically check numerical/metric context, author-claim vs critique boundaries, verified vs inherited citations, and consequential missing qualifiers. Audit whether high/medium upstream critiques or counterexamples are accurately represented or reasonably omitted for concision; classify concern vs demonstrated error before assigning severity.
